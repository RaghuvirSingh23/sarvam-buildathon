## Core idea

Build around Sarvam's official vision, “AI for all from India.” Express breadth,
inclusion, capability, and Indian context without reducing India to decorative
symbols.

The official brand explanation describes the desired balance as grounded,
familiar, modern, disciplined, and technically ambitious. Translate that into
calm layouts, direct language, carefully structured detail, and product
demonstrations that feel useful rather than theatrical.

## Conceptual system

Use these official ideas as design logic:

- **Gateway:** Represent transition and openness: human and machine, culture and
  computation, familiar systems and emerging capability.
- **Mandala construction:** Build complexity from simple repeated geometry.
  Let layers interlock and reveal coherence as a whole.
- **Repeated circles:** Use disciplined circular construction behind the
  monogram and compatible supporting motifs.
- **Continuous spectrum:** Move between blue and orange through light and tone.
  Treat the transition as a signal of flow, energy, and progression.
- **Breadth and depth:** Make the system flexible enough for products,
  research, enterprise, government, and developer audiences while retaining
  one recognizable structure.

Do not turn these ideas into generic “Indian-themed” ornament. Prefer abstract
geometry, rhythm, thresholds, and crafted information structure.

## Marks

The current public identity includes:

- a lowercase, wide `sarvam` wordmark;
- a gateway-like monogram;
- a geometric system derived from repeated circles;
- supporting motifs that use line work, lattice-like repetition, and balanced
  symmetry.

For an officially branded deliverable, use approved original vector assets.
Never typeset a replacement wordmark, trace the monogram, or pull a mark from a
screenshot. Keep clear space around the wordmark and preserve its aspect ratio.

For a Sarvam-aligned but unofficial product, omit the marks. Create a distinct
product identity while retaining the layout, typography, palette, and motion
logic.

## Art direction

Prefer:

- warm off-white or white surfaces with areas of very soft color;
- orange-to-indigo atmospheric transitions;
- fine geometric motifs at low visual weight;
- ample empty space and centered editorial compositions;
- large, well-cropped product visuals;
- monochrome proof logos when their use is authorized;
- subtle grain only inside cinematic or campaign panels;
- abstract diagrams that imply systems, networks, or progression.

Avoid:

- saffron-only palettes;
- dense ornamental borders around every component;
- tourist imagery or region-specific symbolism used without product relevance;
- neon cyberpunk styling;
- default startup gradients with no structural purpose;
- mixing several unrelated illustration styles.

## Voice and copy

Use short declarative headlines. Follow them with plain-language proof or a
specific product capability.

Good patterns:

- “Built for the way India speaks.”
- “From document to decision.”
- “Deploy where your data lives.”
- “One workflow. Every language.”

Treat those as structural examples, not required copy. Keep claims
evidence-based. Pair human outcomes with credible technical detail. Prefer
“what it enables” over a list of adjectives.

Use compact uppercase labels for categories and metadata. Use sentence case for
headings, controls, and navigation. Keep primary navigation nouns short.

## Official versus inspired usage

Exact font families, logos, customer marks, imagery, and some public assets may
be protected. Public visibility is not a license to redistribute or hotlink
them.

Use exact assets only when the buildathon or product owner has permission.
Otherwise:

- replace the logo with the product's own identity;
- use a licensed display serif/sans mix with similar proportions;
- recreate gradients from recorded color tokens instead of copying image files;
- use original motifs built from simple geometry;
- preserve the design logic without implying a Sarvam partnership.
